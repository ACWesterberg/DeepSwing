# No-model backtest of the post-mortem rules — 2026-10-27

Run on the Pi with `scripts/backtest_rules.py` (branch `claude/eloquent-wright-bybwvi`), 2023-01-01 → 2026-10-24, four ~yearly windows, each starting from 100,000 SEK. No LLM is involved: every screener survivor that passes risk validation is bought at the next day's open with a 2×ATR stop and a `min_rrr` target.

## Why

The Aug–Oct 2026 live run went 8 wins / 75 trades (−39k SEK, ~25k of it a pence/pound bug). The question was whether the rules underneath the models ever had an edge.

## Old rules vs new rules

`legacy` approximates the gates and exits that traded live (momentum after a volume spike, RSI ≤ 78, ATR ≥ 2%, no liquidity floor, ~1×ATR stop, breakeven at 1×ATR, RRR 2.5). `current` is the rebuilt rule set. Mean R per trade with a 95% interval:

| Market | Rules | Trades | Win | Mean R | 95% CI | Mean R by year (2023 / 24 / 25 / 26) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nordic | legacy | 2,694 | 28% | −0.12 | −0.18 … −0.06 | −0.11 / −0.17 / −0.17 / −0.04 |
| Nordic | current | 714 | 39% | **+0.09** | +0.00 … +0.19 | −0.02 / +0.23 / +0.09 / +0.10 |
| US | legacy | 2,859 | 31% | −0.10 | −0.15 … −0.04 | −0.06 / −0.03 / −0.09 / −0.20 |
| US | current | 986 | 39% | **+0.06** | −0.03 … +0.14 | +0.01 / +0.14 / +0.03 / +0.08 |
| EU | legacy | 2,522 | 30% | −0.23 | −0.31 … −0.15 | −0.28 / −0.20 / −0.06 / −0.38 |
| EU | current | 906 | 37% | −0.05 | −0.14 … +0.04 | −0.20 / −0.00 / +0.10 / −0.08 |

The legacy rules lose with statistical confidence in every market, before any model is involved — consistent with the live result. The current rules are positive on Nordic and US in 3–4 of 4 years, but the edge is thin: Nordic's interval only just excludes zero and US's does not.

## Exit variants (current rules, one setting changed)

| Variant | Nordic | US | EU |
|---|---|---|---|
| default (stop 2×ATR, RRR 2.0, breakeven 2×ATR) | +0.09 | +0.06 | −0.05 |
| `min_rrr` 1.5 | +0.12 | +0.04 | −0.06 |
| `min_rrr` 3.0 | +0.11 | +0.09 | +0.03 |
| stop 1.5×ATR | +0.02 | +0.05 | −0.11 |
| stop 2.5×ATR | +0.08 | +0.11 | −0.01 |
| no breakeven floor | +0.10 | +0.06 | −0.09 |
| breakeven at 1×ATR (old) | +0.07 | +0.03 | −0.07 |

Every variant sits inside the default's ±0.05R standard error and their year-by-year rankings flip, so the defaults stay. Two directions are consistent across all three markets and support the changes already made: a tighter stop (1.5×ATR) is never better, and arming breakeven at 1×ATR is never better than at 2×ATR.

## Does the pullback filter add anything?

`trendonly` drops the RSI band and the spike-day rejection, keeping only uptrend + liquidity + ATR band:

| Market | current | trend-only |
|---|---|---|
| Nordic | +0.09 | +0.09 |
| US | +0.06 | +0.04 |
| EU | −0.05 | −0.02 |

No measurable difference. The improvement over legacy comes from trading only liquid names in established uptrends, stops outside daily noise, a later breakeven, and next-open fills — not from the pullback scoring.

## Decisions taken

- **EU is off** (`market_allocation["eu"] = 0.0`): negative under every variant and badly negative in 2023. Note that omitting a market from `MARKET_ALLOCATION` leaves it *uncapped*; 0.0 disables it.
- Exit defaults unchanged.

## Caveats

- **Survivorship bias.** The universes are today's listings; names delisted or bankrupt during 2023–26 are absent, which flatters a long-only backtest.
- **Market tailwind.** 2023–26 was mostly a rising market. A long-only uptrend filter benefits from that; see the addendum for the buy-and-hold comparison.
- **FX** is converted at one rate for the whole run, and daily bars cannot see intraday order of high and low (stop is assumed first).
- **Returns vs R.** Window returns can disagree in sign with mean R (US 2024–25: +0.03R, −7%) because drawdown mode halves size after losses and position size varies with the value cap; R is the like-for-like measure.
- The model layer is untested here. Any LLM filter should be judged against these rules alone on the same signals: it has to beat roughly +0.06–0.10R, not zero.

## Addendum: can different rules do better? (same day)

Tested on a second, independent period — **2016–2022**, yearly windows — as well as 2023–26. A change was only to be adopted if it improved both Nordic and US in both periods.

**The current rules out of sample.** Nordic 2016–22: 1,668 trades, **+0.02R** (years −0.12 / +0.10 / −0.06 / +0.08 / +0.18 / +0.13 / −0.02). US 2016–22: 1,748 trades, **+0.05R**, consistent with 2023–26. Legacy Nordic 2016–22: −0.11R. So the improvement over the old rules is robust; a positive edge is not — on Nordic it was specific to 2023–26.

**Where the profit sits (2023–26 trades).** The top 10% of trades carry all of it (Nordic +143R vs −75R for the rest; US +219R vs −161R). Half the take-profit exits ran on to 4R+ within 40 days. An index-trend filter would not have helped: entries with the index below its 200-day average did no worse.

**Rule variants, 2023–26 (mean R per trade, total R):**

| Variant | Nordic | US |
|---|---|---|
| current | +0.09 (64R) | +0.06 (57R) |
| no target, 3×ATR trail | +0.11 (67R) | +0.06 (41R) |
| no target, 4×ATR trail | +0.19 (91R) | +0.08 (48R) |
| `min_atr_pct` 2% | +0.06 | +0.06 |
| no target + 2% ATR | +0.10 | +0.06 |

Letting winners run with a 4×ATR trail lifts Nordic but not US (and US swings +0.52 / −0.21R between years), so it fails the bar; the ATR floor does nothing. Not adopted.

**Against simply holding the liquid universe equal-weight** (same data, same liquidity floor, monthly rebalance): Nordic +8.2%/yr (2023–26) and +6.0%/yr (2016–22); US +8.2% and +10.4%. The swing rules' average yearly window return was about +9.5%, +1.6%, +2% and +11% respectively — at best level with doing nothing, at lower exposure.

**Monthly 12-1 momentum** (top-N by trailing 12-month return, skipping the last month) vs that equal-weight benchmark:

| | 2016–22 | 2023–26 |
|---|---|---|
| Nordic, top 20 (≥10 MSEK/day) | +7.7% vs +6.0%/yr, maxDD −36% | +11.6% vs +8.2%, maxDD −18% |
| US, top 20 (≥10 MSEK/day) | — | **−13.7%** vs +8.2%, maxDD −60% |
| US, top 50 (≥100 MSEK/day), fixed before the 2016–22 run | +15.3% vs +11.2%, maxDD −43% | +19.9% vs +12.3%, maxDD −28% |

The US 2016–22 excess is almost entirely 2020 (+76 points); momentum lagged in 3 of 7 years (2021: −33 points). It is a bet on rare trend years with deeper drawdowns, not a dependable improvement.

**Conclusion.** No rule change tested gives a robust gain, and the current swing rules do not reliably beat holding the same liquid stocks. With these data and costs, daily-bar technical swing rules carry roughly zero edge once the obvious errors are removed.
