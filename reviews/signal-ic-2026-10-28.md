# Which signals predict returns? Cross-sectional IC study (2026-10-28)

`scripts/signal_ic.py` on the cached universes. Universe: names with median daily turnover ≥ 10 MSEK and price ≥ 10 SEK. Every 5th trading day, each signal (known at that day's close) is rank-correlated with each liquid stock's return from the next open to the close h days later. On average that is about 175–210 Nordic names, 2,200–3,000 US and 780 EU per cross-section. t-stats are Newey–West, to account for overlapping windows. "Top-quintile excess" is the average return of the fifth of stocks with the highest signal value, minus the liquid-universe average, per h days.

This scores a signal on every stock-day rather than on the ~700 trades a rule happens to take, which is how systematic funds evaluate ideas. Typical useful ICs at these horizons are 0.02–0.08.

## What it says

1. **Volatility is the most reliable signal, and it points away.** Volatile, lottery-like names (high ATR, big recent daily spikes) underperform in every market and period, by about 1–2% per 60 days for the most volatile fifth. That is the low-volatility / MAX anomaly. The live run's original screener *rewarded* exactly these names.
2. **Proximity to the 52-week high** is the most consistent positive signal: same sign in all five cells and significant in all four core cells at 60 days. This replicates George & Hwang (2004) on our data.
3. **12-1 momentum** helps at 60 days in 2023–26 and in EU, but not in 2016–22. **Size/liquidity** helps, mostly in the US.
4. **The swing-timing toolbox predicts nothing.** RSI(14), RSI(2), IBS, 1-week and 1-month returns, distance from the 50-day average, and EMA21 over SMA50 all flip sign and stay inside ±2 t across cells, at 5 and 20 days alike. Short-term momentum in high-turnover names (Medhat & Schmeling) does not replicate here.
5. **The current screener gates do select slightly better stocks** (IC +0.013 to +0.029, positive in all five cells). That matches the backtests' small positive edge. Almost all of it comes from the trend and volatility conditions, not the RSI or volume conditions.
6. **The predictability is mostly on the downside.** Out of sample, the US composite ranks stocks well (IC +0.066, t 3.0), yet its top fifth barely beats the average: it works by identifying losers. For a long-only account it is a *filter* (avoid the volatile junk), not a stock picker. Nordic's composite does both, modestly: about +0.46% per 60 days for the top fifth, ≈ 2% a year before costs.

## Implications for rules

- Rank and filter on slow, structural signals: nearness to the 52-week high, low volatility, and liquidity. Reconsider the daily oscillator gates (RSI band, EMA21 > SMA50), which carry no measurable information.
- The predictive horizon is 1–3 months, not days. That favours fewer, longer holds and periodic rebalancing over daily entries and tight targets, which also cuts costs.
- The edge is a couple of percent a year over holding the liquid universe, before costs. It is worth having only with low turnover. Shorting the bottom fifth would be where most of the spread is, but that is impractical for this account.
- Next test: a long-only, low-turnover portfolio backtest that ranks by near-52-week-high plus low volatility (signals fixed by the 2016–22 selection), holds about 20–30 names, rebalances every 2–4 weeks with a turnover buffer, and is compared with equal-weight holding net of costs in both periods.
