# Which signals predict returns, and does a portfolio of them pay? (2026-10-28/29)

Two tools on the cached universes. Both use the same liquid universe: median daily turnover ≥ 10 MSEK and price ≥ 10 SEK.

- `scripts/signal_ic.py` scores signals. Every 5th trading day, each signal (known at that day's close) is rank-correlated with every liquid stock's return from the next open to the close h days later. On average that is about 190–230 Nordic names, 2,200–3,000 US and 780 EU per cross-section. t-stats are Newey–West, to account for overlapping windows. "Top-quintile excess" is the fifth of stocks with the highest signal value, minus the liquid-universe average, per h days. Typical useful ICs at these horizons are 0.02–0.08.
- `scripts/portfolio_backtest.py` tests the long-only book those signals suggest, against the equal-weight liquid universe, net of costs.

**Correction (2026-10-29).** The first Nordic and EU runs had a panel bug. On a day only another exchange traded, a stock's volume was NaN, which blanked its 20-day turnover window, so stocks fell out of the liquid universe for a month after every exchange-specific holiday. Fixed in `cae0dbe`. The Nordic and EU numbers below are the corrected ones; US (one calendar) was unaffected.

## Portfolio test (pre-registered)

Main specification, fixed before any portfolio run:
- **Ranking:** equal-weight percentile rank of nearness to the 52-week high and low 60-day volatility.
- **Book:** top 25, equal weight. Rebalance every 20 trading days; a holding is kept while it ranks inside the top 50. Decide at the close, fill at the next open.
- **Costs:** 0.15% per side, 0.25% with FX on US/EU.
- **Benchmark:** the equal-weight liquid universe on the same schedule and costs.
- **Pass mark:** beat the benchmark's CAGR in Nordic and US, in both periods.

**Verdict: fails the pre-registered bar.** It loses to equal-weight holding in US in both periods and in Nordic 2016–22. What it does deliver everywhere is lower risk: volatility about 15–50% lower and shallower drawdowns. Sharpe is higher in Nordic (both periods) and EU, about equal in US. It behaves as a defensive tilt that gives up return in sharp rebounds (2020: −26 points) and earns it back in falling or narrow markets (2022: +9 points). The 52-week-high-only variant beat the benchmark in both Nordic periods, but it was not the pre-registered specification and turns over 5–11× a year. It is a hypothesis for fresh data, not a result.

## What it all says

1. **Volatility predicts, pointing away.** Volatile, lottery-like names (high ATR, big recent daily spikes) underperform in every market and period: significant in all four core cells at both horizons, about 1–2% per 60 days for the most volatile fifth. The live run's original screener rewarded exactly these names.
2. **Nearness to the 52-week high** is the most consistent positive signal (George & Hwang 2004 replicates here). 12-1 momentum helps at 60 days in 2023–26 and EU. Size/liquidity helps, mostly in the US.
3. **The swing-timing toolbox carries no information.** RSI(14), RSI(2), IBS, 1-week and 1-month returns, distance from the 50-day average and EMA21 over SMA50 all stay inside ±2 t and flip sign across cells.
4. **Predictability is mostly on the downside.** The signals sort losers well (the US composite holds out of sample, IC +0.066, t 3.0) but barely lift the top fifth. A long-only book can only use that by avoiding names, which tilts it defensive rather than ahead.
5. **So a long-only ranked book is a lower-risk way to hold stocks, not a way to beat holding them.** With the evidence here, the reliable use of these signals in DeepSwing is as a filter: never buy the most volatile or lottery-like names, prefer names near their highs. That improves risk-adjusted results; it does not reliably add return. Equal-weight holding of the liquid universe (+9–11% a year here) is the bar any active rule has to clear, and nothing tested so far clears it consistently.

## Caveats

- Survivorship bias: the universes are today's listings. It flatters equal-weight holding and the strategy alike, so the comparison between them is fairer than either level.
- Costs exclude minimum ticket fees, which matter for a 100k SEK book split 25 ways.
- Two periods, the second only about 3.7 years. A third period (e.g. 2009–15) would be needed before trusting any variant chosen after seeing these.
